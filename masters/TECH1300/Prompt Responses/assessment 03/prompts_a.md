# Assessment Prompts — A

## A1 — How e-Commerce supports business activities

### Prompt

**Role:** Act as a researcher and Information System specialist

**Context:** We are producing information to support a report for an academic assignment that evaluates how e-Commerce supports business activities

**Task:** Give examples of how e-Commerce systems can support business activities. Think of the most important business activities put it in a ranking list and show how e-Commerce addressees each activity.

**Scope:** Explain each activity with a brief overview. Which roles e-Commerce systems plays in the activity. What are the benefits, challenges and possible limitations with improvement suggestions.

**Criterion:** Consult data available publicly on internet: industry reports, reviews, platform features, researches in order to build an accurate evaluation of the most important business activities considering stakeholders, ESG impact and business effectiveness.

**Format:** List of business activities with respective examples of how e-commerce systems support their operations.

### Response

E-Commerce systems support business activities by connecting customer transactions with inventory, fulfilment, marketing, financial records, and management information. Their value depends on how well the technology fits the organisation’s processes, employees, and objectives.

The following ranking assumes a **small or medium-sized retailer selling physical products online**. It prioritises activities that enable revenue and reliable service, followed by activities that improve growth and decision-making. Stakeholder interests and environmental, social, and governance (**ESG**) considerations inform each evaluation. **This is an analytical ranking, not a published industry league table**; priorities would differ for a service business or digital-product supplier.

Platform documentation establishes available capabilities, while OECD and UNCTAD research provides broader context. The business examples below are illustrative, and the suggested benefits are potential outcomes rather than guaranteed results.

1. **Sales and order processing**

   **Overview and system role:** This activity converts customer demand into recorded orders. An e-Commerce system presents products, accepts orders, connects to payment services, and communicates order status. Shopify, for example, supports payment capture, order editing, confirmations, and fulfilment preparation. [Shopify: Managing orders](https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/fulfillment/managing-orders)

   **Example:** A clothing retailer receives an online order outside store hours. The system records the selected size, delivery address, and payment status so employees can prepare the order.

   **Benefits:** Greater purchasing convenience, less manual data entry, and a consistent transaction record for customers and employees. Online platforms can also help smaller firms reach customers beyond their local markets. [OECD: SMEs in the online platform economy](https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/the-digital-transformation-of-smes_bdb9256a-en/full-report/component-7.html)

   **Challenges and limitations:** Technical failures can interrupt sales, incorrect product information can create disputes, and payment or platform costs affect margins. An online storefront does not automatically generate customer demand.

   **Improvements and ESG considerations:** Test checkout accessibility, display total costs clearly, maintain accurate product descriptions, and establish procedures for failed payments. These measures support customer inclusion and transparent business conduct.

   **Useful measures:** Checkout completion rate, order error rate, and contribution margin per order.

2. **Inventory management and replenishment**

   **Overview and system role:** Inventory management ensures products are available without tying up excessive cash in unsold stock. Shopify supports inventory tracking, quantity adjustments, and inventory reports. Low-stock notifications require an alert app or a configured Shopify Flow workflow. [Shopify: Managing inventory](https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/products/inventory)

   **Example:** A retailer tracks shirts by size and colour, using sales and stock information to decide which variants to reorder.

   **Benefits:** Better visibility for purchasing and sales employees, fewer avoidable stockouts, and more informed replenishment decisions.

   **Challenges and limitations:** Recorded stock may differ from physical stock because of damage, theft, or incorrect entries. Connecting online and physical-store inventory requires compatible systems and reliable synchronisation. Inventory software cannot resolve supplier shortages by itself.

   **Improvements and ESG considerations:** Combine stock tracking with physical counts, supplier lead-time records, and human review of reorder decisions. Reducing avoidable overstock can limit waste, while supplier selection should also consider labour practices and product traceability.

   **Useful measures:** Stockout rate, inventory accuracy, stock turnover, and obsolete-stock value.

3. **Order fulfilment and delivery coordination**

   **Overview and system role:** Fulfilment turns a recorded order into a delivered product. E-Commerce systems exchange order information with warehouses, carriers, or fulfilment providers. Fulfilment by Amazon illustrates this integration: participating sellers send inventory to Amazon, which handles picking, packing, shipping, customer service, and returns. [Amazon: Fulfilment by Amazon](https://sell.amazon.com/fulfillment-by-amazon)

   **Example:** A small retailer outsources fulfilment instead of operating its own warehouse, while monitoring order progress through connected systems.

   **Benefits:** Access to logistics capacity and less internal coordination work. Customers benefit when delivery information is accurate and orders arrive reliably.

   **Challenges and limitations:** Outsourcing introduces fees and dependence on provider performance. The merchant has less direct control over packing and handling, and digital tracking cannot prevent physical transport disruptions.

   **Improvements and ESG considerations:** Compare total fulfilment costs, monitor service performance, and offer consolidated delivery where practical. Assess packaging, transport emissions, and worker conditions alongside speed. UNCTAD explains that rapid delivery can increase vehicle movements and reduce load efficiency, making environmental outcomes dependent on delivery choices. [UNCTAD: Digital Economy Report 2024, Chapter V](https://unctad.org/system/files/information-document/tihe2024_d_01_en.pdf)

   **Useful measures:** On-time delivery, cost per order, damage rate, and packaging weight per shipment.

4. **Customer service and returns management**

   **Overview and system role:** This activity resolves questions, complaints, exchanges, and refunds. E-Commerce records give employees access to transaction details. Shopify supports returns, exchanges, refunds, and configurable self-service return requests. [Shopify: Managing orders](https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/fulfillment/managing-orders)

   **Example:** A customer requests a different clothing size through an order page. An employee reviews the request and coordinates the exchange.

   **Benefits:** More consistent case handling, less repeated information gathering, and clearer communication between customers and staff.

   **Challenges and limitations:** Automated processes may struggle with unusual complaints. A return request does not establish the physical condition of an item, and generous return arrangements can increase handling costs.

   **Improvements and ESG considerations:** Provide human escalation, clear return information, and accessible support channels. Record return reasons to improve product descriptions and sizing guidance. Inspect returned goods for resale or repair: returns can generate additional transport and packaging impacts. [UNCTAD: E-Commerce and environmental sustainability](https://unctad.org/system/files/official-document/der2024_ch05_en.pdf)

   **Useful measures:** Resolution time, customer satisfaction, return rate by reason, and the proportion of returned products recovered for resale.

5. **Marketing and customer retention**

   **Overview and system role:** Marketing attracts customers and encourages repeat purchases. E-Commerce systems can connect purchasing data with customer segmentation and communication tools. Shopify supports rule-based customer segments and marketing workflows, including messages to customers who have subscribed. [Shopify: Marketing automation tools](https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/promoting-marketing/create-marketing/automation-tools)

   **Example:** A retailer sends a relevant seasonal offer to subscribed customers in a particular location, rather than sending the same campaign to its entire database.

   **Benefits:** More relevant communication, less repetitive campaign administration, and opportunities to evaluate customer responses.

   **Challenges and limitations:** Incomplete data can produce inappropriate targeting. Discounts may increase sales while reducing profit, and platform-attributed sales do not necessarily prove that a campaign caused those purchases.

   **Improvements and ESG considerations:** Limit unnecessary data collection, provide clear communication preferences, and test campaigns against comparison groups where feasible. Avoid manipulative messages, misleading sustainability claims, and targeting that unfairly excludes customers.

   **Useful measures:** Customer acquisition cost, repeat purchase rate, unsubscribe rate, and campaign contribution after costs.

6. **Financial administration and reconciliation**

   **Overview and system role:** Financial administration connects orders with payments, refunds, fees, and bank deposits. Payment services integrated with e-Commerce systems support this process. Stripe’s payout reconciliation report, for example, identifies transactions associated with automatic payouts. Manual and instant payouts require different reconciliation approaches. [Stripe: Payout reconciliation](https://docs.stripe.com/reports/payout-reconciliation)

   **Example:** A bookkeeper checks why a bank deposit is lower than recorded sales by reviewing processing fees and refunds.

   **Benefits:** Less manual matching, better visibility of cash movements, and clearer records for managers and accounting staff.

   **Challenges and limitations:** Sales, profit, and available cash are different measures. Payout timing, disputes, and integration errors can complicate reconciliation. A payment report does not replace a complete accounting system.

   **Improvements and ESG considerations:** Reconcile regularly, investigate exceptions, and restrict refund permissions. These controls strengthen governance by improving accountability and reducing opportunities for errors or misuse.

   **Useful measures:** Unreconciled transactions, reconciliation time, payment fees, and overdue exceptions.

7. **Performance monitoring and business planning**

   **Overview and system role:** This activity turns operational records into information for decisions. Shopify’s order analytics includes order volumes, returns, fulfilled orders, and time to fulfil. [Shopify: Viewing order analytics](https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/fulfillment/managing-orders/analytics/order-analytics)

   **Example:** A manager investigates a product with strong sales but frequent returns before deciding whether to reorder it.

   **Benefits:** Greater visibility of operational problems and a common evidence base for purchasing, service, and management teams.

   **Challenges and limitations:** Dashboards reflect the data collected and may omit external costs or offline activity. Historical patterns may not predict future demand, and a correlation does not establish a cause.

   **Improvements and ESG considerations:** Combine sales information with costs, return reasons, supplier performance, and customer feedback. Include waste, accessibility complaints, and delivery impacts so decisions reflect stakeholder outcomes as well as revenue.

   **Useful measures:** Margin after returns, forecast accuracy, customer retention, and waste per order.

**Security and governance underpin every activity in this ranking.** Access controls, staff training, backups, and multifactor authentication should accompany implementation. NIST recommends these measures as ongoing business practices, rather than one-time technical fixes. [NIST: Cybersecurity Basics](https://www.nist.gov/itl/smallbusinesscyber/cybersecurity-basics)

The evidence also has limits: platform documentation demonstrates features but does not independently establish business results. OECD identifies skills, funding, and resource constraints as barriers to successful SME digitalisation. Therefore, a business should test whether the selected system improves its own operating measures before assuming that automation will deliver the expected benefits. [OECD: The Digital Transformation of SMEs](https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/the-digital-transformation-of-smes_bdb9256a-en/full-report.html)

## A3 — Social, privacy and security concerns in e-Commerce

### Prompt

**Role:** Act as a researcher and Information System specialist

**Context:** You are supporting a report for an academic assignment about Information Systems

**Task:** Explain social, privacy and security concerns in e-Commerce transactions and user data handling

**Scope:** Make the topics: Social, privacy and security, explain their importance, key concerns, challenges and risks, then relate each of them to e-Commerce transactions and user data handling.

**Criterion:** Consult data available publicly on internet: industry reports, reviews, platform features, researches in order to produce more accurate insights and find how companies are addressing those concerns.

**Format:** Summarise each topic into paragraphs with the same structure and provide a final conclusion paragraph.

### Response

**Social concerns.** Social responsibility in e-Commerce concerns whether people can participate fairly, make informed purchasing decisions, and avoid exploitation. Key concerns include inaccessible websites, differences in digital skills, and manipulative interfaces. For transactions, inaccessible checkout forms can prevent customers with disabilities from completing purchases; W3C guidance therefore emphasises keyboard access, understandable forms, and compatibility with assistive technologies. [W3C: Accessibility Principles](https://www.w3.org/WAI/fundamentals/accessibility-principles/). For user data handling, deceptive interfaces can pressure customers into sharing information or accepting tracking. OECD research identifies false urgency, misleading social proof, and difficult cancellation processes as examples of harmful commercial practices. [OECD: Dark commercial patterns](https://www.oecd.org/en/topics/dark-commercial-patterns.html). The business challenge is balancing sales targets with customer autonomy and inclusion: increasing conversion does not necessarily indicate a fair customer experience. Companies can respond through accessible design, transparent offers, straightforward cancellation, and user testing with diverse customers. For example, Shopify provides merchants with guidance on image descriptions, readable text, and keyboard navigation, while explicitly stating that following its guidance alone does not guarantee full accessibility. [Shopify: Accessibility for themes](https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/online-store/themes/customizing-themes/accessibility). These measures help address exclusion and manipulation, but require ongoing evaluation as storefronts change.

**Privacy concerns.** Privacy concerns whether personal information is collected, used, shared, and retained appropriately. It matters because e-Commerce combines transaction information—such as names, delivery addresses, and purchase histories—with behavioural information that can reveal customer interests. Key risks include excessive collection, unclear purposes, unexpected profiling, and sharing information with third parties beyond customers’ expectations. For example, collecting an address to deliver an order does not automatically justify every subsequent advertising use. In Australia, organisations covered by the Australian Privacy Principles must consider requirements governing collection, notification, use, disclosure, and security; OAIC guidance emphasises collecting information reasonably necessary for business activities and using lawful, fair collection methods. [OAIC: Australian Privacy Principles](https://www.oaic.gov.au/privacy/australian-privacy-principles/australian-privacy-principles-guidelines), [OAIC: Collection guidance](https://www.oaic.gov.au/privacy/australian-privacy-principles/australian-privacy-principles-guidelines/chapter-3-app-3-collection-of-solicited-personal-information). The practical challenge is maintaining these controls across payment providers, delivery services, analytics tools, and marketing applications. Companies can respond by minimising collection, explaining purposes, managing applicable choices and permissions, and establishing retention and request-handling procedures. Shopify, for example, provides tools for customer data access and erasure requests, but merchants remain responsible for contacting other companies with which they have shared data. Certain transaction information can remain after personal details are erased. [Shopify: Processing customer data requests](https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/privacy-and-security/privacy/processing-customer-data-requests). Platform tools therefore support privacy management without replacing organisational responsibility.

**Security concerns.** Security protects the confidentiality, integrity, and availability of e-Commerce systems and information: preventing unauthorised disclosure, improper changes, and disruption. Key concerns include phishing, stolen credentials, vulnerable software, excessive access privileges, and ransomware. In transactions, a compromised merchant account could enable unauthorised order changes or fraudulent activity; in data handling, it could expose customer contact details and purchase records. Service disruption can also prevent checkout or delay fulfilment. The challenge is protecting the entire chain of staff accounts, applications, integrations, and service providers while maintaining usable purchasing processes. NIST recommends ongoing controls including multifactor authentication, software updates, staff training, and protected, tested backups. [NIST: Cybersecurity Basics](https://www.nist.gov/itl/smallbusinesscyber/cybersecurity-basics). Companies provide mechanisms to support these controls: Shopify allows merchants to review login history and enforce two-step authentication, while WooCommerce advises backing up stores and testing updates before applying them to the live environment. [Shopify: User security settings](https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/your-account/users/security), [WooCommerce: Update guidance](https://woocommerce.com/document/how-to-update-woocommerce/). Businesses should combine these capabilities with restricted access, monitoring, and incident-response procedures. Their effectiveness depends on implementation and maintenance; the availability of security features does not establish that a particular store is secure.

**Conclusion.** Social responsibility, privacy, and security address different but connected aspects of trustworthy e-Commerce. A technically secure store can still manipulate customers or collect unnecessary information, while a transparent privacy policy cannot prevent attacks without effective controls. Businesses should therefore evaluate the full customer journey—from browsing and consent choices to payment, delivery, and data deletion—and assign responsibility for both platform configuration and staff practices. The company examples above demonstrate available tools and guidance, rather than proof that every merchant implements them effectively.

## A4 — Applicability of SDLC to e-Commerce solutions

### Prompt

**Role:** Act as a researcher and Information System specialist

**Context:** You are supporting a report for an academic assignment about Information Systems and applicability of Software development life-cycle to e-commerce solutions.

**Task:** Give examples of how Software Development Life Cycle (SDLC) can be applied in e-Commerce System Development

**Scope:** Explain how each of the SDLC phases can be applied and how they support system development for e-Commerces.

**Criterion:** Consult data available publicly on internet: industry reports, reviews, platform features, researches in order to identify how leading e-commerce companies are applying SDLC to their solutions.

**Format:** Table explaining each: SLCD stage → key e-commerce domain challenge → example of practical applicability on e-Commerce. Include also a final conclusion with an insight.

### Response

The **Software Development Life Cycle (SDLC)** provides a structured process for planning, building, testing, deploying, and improving e-Commerce systems. The table uses seven phases, separating requirements analysis from planning. In practice, teams can repeat these activities in short development cycles.

The examples below connect documented company practices to SDLC phases; this mapping does not imply that every company follows the same formal lifecycle.

| SDLC stage | Key e-Commerce domain challenge | Example of practical applicability in e-Commerce |
|---|---|---|
| **1. Planning and feasibility** — Define the business problem, expected value, scope, budget, and constraints. This helps prioritise worthwhile investment. | Choosing improvements that benefit customers while remaining affordable and operationally feasible. For example, offering faster delivery requires both software capabilities and fulfilment capacity. | **Practical application:** Before developing same-day delivery, a retailer could assess customer demand, delivery coverage, integration costs, and expected profitability. **Company evidence:** Amazon’s *Working Backwards* approach starts with the intended customer experience. Teams develop a proposed press release and FAQ that examine benefits, costs, and implementation difficulties before committing resources. This illustrates how early evaluation can prevent investment in poorly justified features. [Amazon: Working Backwards](https://www.aboutamazon.com/news/workplace/an-insider-look-at-amazons-culture-and-processes). |
| **2. Requirements analysis** — Establish what the system must do and the quality standards it must meet. Clear acceptance criteria guide development and testing. | Reconciling customer convenience with merchant needs, accessibility, performance, and security. Vague requirements such as “make checkout better” are difficult to verify. | **Practical application:** Define functional requirements, such as discount calculation and order confirmation, alongside measurable performance targets and keyboard accessibility requirements. **Company evidence:** Shopify’s account of its extension framework identifies requirements including consistent user experiences, accessibility, performance, developer usability, and secure execution of third-party code. These demonstrate how requirements extend beyond visible shopping features. [Shopify: Extensible UI](https://shopify.engineering/remote-rendering-ui-extensibility). |
| **3. System design** — Specify the architecture, interfaces, data structures, and user journeys that will satisfy requirements. | Connecting storefronts, payments, inventory, and third-party applications without allowing one component to undermine checkout reliability or expose unnecessary data. | **Practical application:** Design how stock is reserved, payment results update orders, and failed transactions release reservations. Define which information each integration can access. **Company evidence:** Shopify describes a remote-rendering architecture that separates third-party extension code into a restricted sandbox while the host controls interface rendering. This connects design decisions to requirements for extensibility, consistency, and isolation. [Shopify: Remote-rendering architecture](https://shopify.engineering/remote-rendering-ui-extensibility). |
| **4. Development and integration** — Implement features, configure platform capabilities, and connect services. Code review and reusable components support maintainability. | Changing checkout functionality while preserving existing merchant configurations and compatibility with platform APIs. | **Practical application:** Build and review small increments, such as address validation or delivery selection, with shared components for recurring behaviour. **Company evidence:** In its 2026 Checkout Blocks upgrade, Shopify migrated five extensions incrementally, starting with the smallest and lowest-risk. A new shared library operated alongside the existing library until migration was complete. This illustrates how manageable implementation steps support a complex upgrade. [Shopify: Checkout Blocks upgrade](https://shopify.engineering/upgrading-checkout-blocks-app-to-polaris-web-components). |
| **5. Testing and validation** — Check that the system meets functional and quality requirements before and during release. | Errors can create incorrect charges, overselling, inaccessible checkout, or outages during sales peaks. Individual components may work while their combined purchasing journey fails. | **Practical application:** Test complete purchase and refund journeys, failed payments, simultaneous stock purchases, accessibility, security, and peak demand. **Company evidence:** Shopify’s published performance-testing account describes simulated browsing, cart, checkout, merchant administration, and flash-sale activity. Teams use results to identify weaknesses and repeat improvements. Shopify also acknowledges that synthetic load tests cannot guarantee identical performance under real traffic. [Shopify: Performance testing at scale](https://shopify.engineering/scale-performance-testing). |
| **6. Deployment** — Release validated changes with monitoring and recovery arrangements. This controls the transition into live operation. | Introducing updates without disrupting purchases or damaging order data. Problems may emerge only under production conditions. | **Practical application:** Prepare release instructions, verify configuration, monitor transaction failures, and establish a recovery plan that preserves newly received orders. **Company evidence:** WooCommerce’s documented release process includes staging deployment and monitoring before final publication. Critical issues trigger a staging revert and pause the release process; monitoring continues after release. [WooCommerce: Building and publishing a release](https://developer.woocommerce.com/docs/contribution/releases/building-and-publishing/). |
| **7. Maintenance and continuous improvement** — Monitor operation, resolve defects, update dependencies, and feed findings into future requirements. | Evolving customer expectations, security vulnerabilities, API changes, and extension incompatibilities can reduce reliability after launch. | **Practical application:** Track checkout errors, investigate customer complaints, apply updates, and retest affected purchasing journeys. **Platform guidance:** WooCommerce advises merchants to back up their stores and test updates in a staging environment before applying them to live sites. This illustrates ongoing maintenance responsibilities at the merchant level, alongside the platform provider’s development work. [WooCommerce: Update guidance](https://woocommerce.com/document/how-to-update-woocommerce/). |

Security should run through every phase: requirements define protection needs, design establishes controls, development implements them, testing checks them, and maintenance addresses emerging vulnerabilities. NIST’s Secure Software Development Framework explicitly recommends integrating security practices into the chosen SDLC. [NIST SP 800-218](https://doi.org/10.6028/NIST.SP.800-218).

**Conclusion.** SDLC supports e-Commerce by connecting business objectives to reliable implementation and ongoing improvement. The central insight is that deployment begins another cycle of learning: transaction failures, customer feedback, and performance measurements should shape the next requirements. Success should therefore be evaluated through outcomes such as accurate orders, dependable checkout, and effective recovery. The company sources provide concrete examples of engineering practices, although they are self-reported accounts rather than independent proof of effectiveness across every implementation.

## A5 — Requirements gathering techniques for e-Commerce systems

### Prompt

**Role:** Act as a researcher and Information System specialist

**Format:** A “0 to 5” stars ranking of the best techniques and a final conclusion paragraph about them.

### Response

**Interviews, collaborative workshops, and prototype-based usability testing are the strongest starting combination for gathering e-Commerce requirements.** Each reveals different needs: customer expectations, business rules, and difficulties completing shopping tasks.

The ratings below are **my assessment of practical suitability**, based on depth of insight, stakeholder coverage, and ability to produce actionable requirements. They are not measured adoption rates. The scale runs from **0 = unsuitable to 5 = highly valuable**; equal scores indicate complementary techniques.

| Technique and rating | Recommended for | Strengths | Weaknesses | Importance for e-Commerce and practical example |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Stakeholder interviews — ★★★★★ 5/5** | Early discovery; understanding customers, merchants, support staff, warehouse teams, and payment specialists. | Explore motivations, exceptions, and business rules in depth. Follow-up questions clarify ambiguous requests. | Time-consuming; interviewer bias and inaccurate recollection can distort findings. Small samples may miss customer segments. | Reveal requirements beyond the storefront. Interviewing returns staff could identify a need for partial refunds and separate tracking of returned items. IIBA recognises interviews as a core elicitation technique and emphasises preparation and attentive listening. [IIBA](https://www.iiba.org/business-analysis-blogs/the-five-techniques-you-should-know/). |
| **Collaborative requirements workshops — ★★★★★ 5/5** | Resolving conflicting priorities and defining processes involving several departments. | Bring decision-makers together; expose dependencies; support agreement on scope, priorities, and acceptance criteria. | Scheduling is difficult; dominant participants and groupthink can suppress important concerns. Require skilled facilitation. | Help align marketing promises with payment, inventory, and fulfilment capabilities. For example, a workshop can establish when stock is reserved and what happens after payment failure. IIBA describes workshops as a means of eliciting and confirming stakeholder requirements. [IIBA: Elicitation and collaboration](https://www.iiba.org/professional-development/knowledge-centre/articles/engaging-stakeholders-in-elicitation-and-collaboration/). |
| **Prototyping with usability testing — ★★★★★ 5/5** | Clarifying uncertain requirements for search, product pages, checkout, and returns before substantial development. | Makes abstract ideas concrete; reveals misunderstandings through observed task performance; supports rapid refinement. | Requires representative participants. A convincing prototype can hide technical constraints, and successful test tasks do not guarantee real purchasing behaviour. | Ask shoppers to find a product and complete a simulated checkout. Difficulties can reveal requirements for clearer delivery costs, accessible controls, or guest checkout. Shopify’s guidance explains how task-based testing identifies problems in shopping experiences. [Shopify: User testing](https://www.shopify.com/blog/user-testing). |
| **Observation and contextual inquiry — ★★★★☆ 4/5** | Discovering actual shopping behaviour, fulfilment workflows, and workarounds that people forget to mention. | Reveals implicit needs and environmental constraints; combines observation with clarifying questions. | Resource-intensive; observation may change behaviour; access and participant permission require planning. | Observing staff processing orders could uncover repeated manual copying between systems, suggesting an integration requirement. Shopify’s guidance specifically describes observing customers browsing, adding products, and completing orders in their normal context. [Shopify: Contextual inquiry](https://www.shopify.com/ca/partners/blog/contextual-inquiry). |
| **Existing-data analysis — ★★★★☆ 4/5** | Improving established stores using funnel metrics, search logs, support tickets, and customer feedback. | Reveals recurring problems and their scale; helps prioritise investigations using actual activity. | Data quality and incomplete tracking can mislead. Behavioural patterns rarely explain motivations by themselves; existing data may exclude potential users. | Frequent unsuccessful searches can suggest missing product attributes or synonyms. Repeated delivery complaints can motivate clearer tracking requirements. eBay has documented combining user-activity analysis with interviews to understand commerce experiences. [eBay Research Labs](https://www.ebayinc.com/stories/news/ebays-churchill-integrating-emotions-and-commerce-processes/). |
| **Document and interface analysis — ★★★★☆ 4/5** | Platform migrations, integrations, business-rule discovery, and identifying contractual or technical constraints. | Provides traceable evidence; exposes data fields, dependencies, and exceptions that user research may overlook. | Documents can be outdated, contradictory, or incomplete; written procedures may differ from actual practice. | Reviewing payment-provider specifications, delivery agreements, and returns policies helps define supported transactions and error handling. IIBA recommends checking documents for relevance, credibility, currency, and information gaps. [IIBA: Document analysis](https://www.iiba.org/business-analysis-blogs/the-five-techniques-you-should-know/). |
| **Surveys and questionnaires — ★★★☆☆ 3/5** | Comparing preferences across customer segments after exploratory research has identified useful questions. | Reach many participants efficiently; provide consistent questions and comparable responses. | Self-selection, wording, and non-response bias can distort results. Stated preferences may not predict purchases. | Can assess preferences for delivery options or payment methods. Results should inform prioritisation alongside operational feasibility and observed behaviour. Shopify includes surveys among methods for investigating customer expectations. [Shopify: User research methods](https://www.shopify.com/blog/user-testing). |
| **A/B testing — ★★★☆☆ 3/5 for initial elicitation** | Validating proposed improvements on established stores with sufficient traffic. | Well-designed randomised experiments estimate the effect of a specific change on selected outcomes. | Requires implemented alternatives, adequate sample sizes, and reliable measurement. Does not independently uncover underlying needs. | Compare alternative filter designs and measure successful product discovery. Etsy documents using experiments to inform product-launch decisions. Its strongest role is validating and refining requirements after initial discovery. [Etsy Engineering](https://www.etsy.com/fr/codeascraft/increasing-experimentation-accuracy-and-speed-by-using-control-variates). |

**What does the evidence establish about industry use?** Published examples demonstrate that leading platforms combine techniques. eBay’s research account describes interviews, shopping observation, and activity-data analysis; its search redesign followed 18 months of user research and testing. Etsy documents extensive experimentation. These sources establish use, but do not provide a comparable industry-wide ranking of technique frequency. [eBay: Search redesign](https://innovation.ebayinc.com/stories/ebay-introduces-intuitive-search-redesign-to-elevate-shopper-experience/), [Etsy: Experimentation](https://www.etsy.com/fr/codeascraft/increasing-experimentation-accuracy-and-speed-by-using-control-variates).

Academic evidence provides additional context: Palomares and colleagues’ study of 24 practitioners across 12 Swedish IT companies found meetings and workshops were the most popular elicitation category, except for small projects. However, that sample does not establish adoption rates among leading e-Commerce companies specifically. [Palomares et al., 2021](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00766-020-00345-x).

**Conclusion.** Effective e-Commerce requirements gathering combines techniques according to the uncertainty being addressed. Interviews and observation uncover needs, workshops resolve conflicting priorities, document analysis identifies constraints, and prototypes clarify the proposed experience. Surveys, analytics, and experiments help assess scale and validate improvements. Findings should become prioritised, traceable requirements with acceptance criteria—for example, “Customers can view the total delivery charge before confirming payment”—so that research directly guides development and testing.

## A6 — Impact of trending technologies on e-Commerce operations

### Prompt

**Role:** Act as a researcher and Information System specialist

**Context:** You are supporting a report for an academic assignment about Information Systems and applicability of Software development life-cycle to e-commerce solutions.

**Task:** Evaluate the impact of trending technologies such as AI, Cloud, or Big Data in e-Commerce systems operations.

**Scope:** For each trending technology list which positive impacts they produce, current challenges and concerns, and example of practical applicability and success cases.

**Criterion:** Consult data available publicly on internet: industry reports, reviews, platform features, researches in order to identify which trading technologies are most used by leading e-commerce companies and how they are using it.

**Format:** A list of trending technologies starting by the most trending one first and least trending last. Also, include a final conclusion paragraph summarising the finds.

### Response

**AI, cloud computing, and big data analytics are closely connected technologies transforming e-Commerce operations.** The order below is an analytical ranking of current innovation momentum: AI first, followed by cloud computing and big data analytics. The reviewed sources demonstrate adoption by major platforms but do not provide comparable market-wide adoption rates, so this is not a statistical popularity ranking.

1. **Artificial intelligence — particularly generative and agentic AI**

   **Positive impacts:** AI supports personalised recommendations, conversational product discovery, customer-service automation, fraud detection, and demand forecasting. Generative AI can produce product descriptions and summarise reviews, while agentic AI can perform shopping actions within defined permissions. These capabilities can reduce repetitive work and help customers navigate large catalogues. Reflecting broader momentum, the OECD’s 2025 D4SME survey reported AI use among 39% of respondents. However, its sample was not representative of all SMEs and should not be treated as an e-Commerce adoption rate. [OECD: 2025 survey highlights](https://www.oecd.org/content/dam/oecd/en/networks/oecd-digital-for-smes-global-initiative/D4SME-2025-Policy-Highlights.pdf).

   **Current challenges and concerns:** Incorrect answers can misrepresent products, delivery promises, or refund policies. Personalisation raises privacy and bias concerns, while automated purchasing requires clear authorisation and spending controls. Implementation also requires reliable product data, evaluation, staff training, and ongoing expenditure. NIST identifies risks including fabricated information, privacy exposure, harmful bias, and information-security failures. Businesses should therefore test accuracy, restrict system permissions, and provide human escalation for unresolved cases. [NIST: Generative AI Risk Profile](https://doi.org/10.6028/NIST.AI.600-1).

   **Practical application and reported success:** Amazon’s shopping assistant, originally **Rufus** and renamed **Alexa for Shopping** in May 2026, answers product questions, compares options, and supports shopping actions. Amazon reported that customers using the assistant were over 60% more likely to purchase during that shopping trip. This demonstrates a reported association with purchasing; it does not establish that the assistant caused the increase, because its users may already have stronger purchase intentions. [Amazon: AI shopping assistant](https://www.aboutamazon.com/news/retail/amazon-rufus-ai-assistant-personalized-shopping-features).

2. **Cloud computing — scalable infrastructure and managed services**

   **Positive impacts:** Cloud services provide computing, storage, databases, and application infrastructure that can expand with demand. For e-Commerce, this supports seasonal traffic, international availability, faster deployment, and access to managed analytics and AI services. Smaller businesses can use hosted commerce platforms without operating their own physical servers. The financial benefit depends on workload and configuration; cloud adoption does not automatically reduce total costs.

   **Current challenges and concerns:** Businesses must manage variable usage charges, dependence on providers, service outages, configuration errors, and data-access responsibilities. Applications also need suitable architecture: adding computing capacity cannot automatically resolve an overloaded database or unreliable payment integration. Shopify’s account of its 2025 holiday preparation describes capacity planning, multi-region infrastructure, repeated load tests, and failure simulations—evidence that resilience requires engineering alongside cloud capacity. [Shopify: Preparing for peak demand](https://shopify.engineering/bfcm-readiness-2025).

   **Practical application and reported success:** Shopify uses Google Cloud infrastructure to run commerce services and accommodate traffic peaks. A Google Cloud customer case study reports that Shopify supported **US$14.6 billion in merchant sales** during Black Friday–Cyber Monday 2025, reaching **489 million edge requests per minute** at peak. This demonstrates substantial operational scale, although merchant sales cannot be attributed to cloud technology alone. The source is also a supplier-published case study, rather than an independent evaluation. [Google Cloud: Shopify case study](https://cloud.google.com/customers/shopify-compute).

3. **Big data analytics — integrating and analysing large, diverse datasets**

   **Positive impacts:** Big data systems combine browsing, transactions, inventory, product information, and operational events to improve decisions. Applications include identifying checkout problems, forecasting stock requirements, evaluating promotions, detecting unusual transactions, and supplying reliable data to AI models. Its position here reflects its maturity as an established capability; its operational importance remains high.

   **Current challenges and concerns:** Inconsistent product identifiers, duplicate records, missing events, and outdated information can produce misleading analysis. Combining customer datasets increases privacy and access-control concerns. Large datasets also create storage, processing, and skills costs. Teams need shared definitions, data-quality checks, access restrictions, and retention rules. For example, a sales dashboard should distinguish completed purchases from cancelled or refunded orders.

   **Practical application and reported success:** Etsy uses a managed data architecture to make large datasets accessible across analytics and machine-learning tools. Google Cloud’s case study reports migration of more than **two petabytes of data** and acceleration of data-joining operations by **up to 60%**. This supports faster experimentation and model development for matching shoppers with relevant items. The reported improvement concerns data processing; it should not be interpreted as a 60% improvement in sales or customer experience. [Google Cloud: Etsy data platform](https://cloud.google.com/customers/etsy-lakehouse).

**Conclusion.** AI offers prominent new capabilities for shopping and automation, cloud computing supplies scalable operating infrastructure, and big data analytics provides the information needed for decisions and model development. Their value is strongest when implemented together around measurable business needs. Within the SDLC, organisations should define expected benefits and constraints, test accuracy and resilience, deploy incrementally, and monitor outcomes such as checkout completion, service quality, inventory accuracy, and operating cost. The evidence supports substantial practical benefits, while also showing why company-reported success figures require careful interpretation.
