##### F1 — Business operations and platform strategy

**Reference — APA 7**

OECD. (2021). *The digital transformation of SMEs*. OECD Publishing. [https://doi.org/10.1787/bdb9256a-en](https://doi.org/10.1787/bdb9256a-en)

**Source type:** Research-based professional report.

**Best fit:** Sections 1 and 2.

**Where to read:** Chapter 3, “SMEs in the online platform economy,” particularly Table 3.1, “The main benefits of platforms for SMEs,” and “Risks of lock-ins.” [Read the chapter](https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/the-digital-transformation-of-smes_bdb9256a-en/full-report/component-7.html).

**Evidence:** Platforms can help SMEs access markets, logistics, and business intelligence. However, switching costs, data dependence, and limited resources can constrain the value businesses obtain.

**How to apply it:** Assess whether ChatGPT connects platform capabilities to the retailer’s objectives and resources. If it recommends a platform primarily because it offers many features, investigate whether it overlooks operating costs, data portability, or dependence on the provider.

**Limitation:** This report supports strategic evaluation. It does not verify current Shopify, Amazon, or WooCommerce features or establish that one is universally superior.

**In-text citation:** (OECD, 2021)

##### F2 — Ethics, consumer protection, privacy, and inclusion

**Reference — APA 7**

OECD. (2016). *OECD recommendation of the Council on consumer protection in e-commerce*. OECD Publishing. [https://doi.org/10.1787/9789264255258-en](https://doi.org/10.1787/9789264255258-en)

**Source type:** Professional policy recommendation.

**Best fit:** Section 3; supporting relevance to Section 2.

**Where to read:** Part One, particularly A–B on protection and fair practices, G on privacy and security, and H on digital competence. [Publication and access](https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/oecd-recommendation-of-the-council-on-consumer-protection-in-e-commerce_9789264255258-en.html).

**Evidence:** The recommendation addresses transparent transactions, fair marketing, payment protection, and dispute resolution. Paragraphs 48–49 address fair and transparent data practices and security risk management. It also recognises vulnerable consumers and differences in digital competence.

**How to apply it:** Check whether ChatGPT distinguishes protecting data from using data fairly. A response focused only on encryption may overlook misleading marketing, unclear transaction conditions, or barriers faced by less digitally confident customers.

**Limitation:** This is a normative framework: it describes recommended practices. It is not evidence that a particular company follows them, or a substitute for applicable national law.

**In-text citation:** (OECD, 2016)

##### F3 — Security throughout the SDLC

**Reference — APA 7**

Souppaya, M., Scarfone, K., & Dodson, D. (2022). *Secure software development framework (SSDF) version 1.1: Recommendations for mitigating the risk of software vulnerabilities* (NIST Special Publication 800-218). National Institute of Standards and Technology. [https://doi.org/10.6028/NIST.SP.800-218](https://doi.org/10.6028/NIST.SP.800-218)

**Source type:** Professional technical guidance.

**Best fit:** Section 4; supporting relevance to Sections 3 and 5.

**Where to read:** Table 1, especially PO.1, defining security requirements; PW.8, security testing; and RV practices, responding to vulnerabilities. [Read the publication](https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/SpecialPublications/NIST.SP.800-218.pdf).

**Evidence:** The SSDF integrates security practices into an organisation’s chosen SDLC. It includes identifying and maintaining security requirements, testing software, and responding to vulnerabilities.

**How to apply it:** Evaluate whether ChatGPT treats security as part of requirements, implementation, testing, and maintenance. For an e-Commerce project, examine how its proposed lifecycle addresses checkout changes, customer information, and third-party integrations.

**Limitation:** SSDF complements an SDLC; it is not a replacement lifecycle model. It does not establish that Agile or Waterfall is always the best choice.

**In-text citation:** (Souppaya et al., 2022)

International Institute of Business Analysis. (n.d.). *Engaging stakeholders in elicitation and collaboration*. [Read the article](https://www.iiba.org/professional-development/knowledge-centre/articles/engaging-stakeholders-in-elicitation-and-collaboration/).

##### F5 — AI adoption, business impact, and evidence limitations

**Reference — APA 7**

Bianchini, M., & Lasheras Sancho, M. (2025). *SME digitalisation for competitiveness: The 2025 OECD D4SME survey* (OECD SME and Entrepreneurship Papers No. 68). OECD Publishing. [https://doi.org/10.1787/197e3077-en](https://doi.org/10.1787/197e3077-en)

**Source type:** Empirical professional research report.

**Best fit:** Section 6; supporting relevance to Section 1.

**Where to read:** “The sample,” and Chapter 3, “Use and impact of Artificial Intelligence,” particularly printed page 17. [Read the report](https://www.oecd.org/content/dam/oecd/en/publications/reports/2025/04/sme-digitalisation-for-competitiveness_3116862a/197e3077-en.pdf).

**Evidence:** The survey collected responses from 1,009 SMEs across ten countries. Of surveyed businesses, 39% reported using AI. Among AI users, 43% reported a moderate business impact, 18% a significant impact, and 6% a transformational impact.

**How to apply it:** Challenge claims that adopting AI automatically transforms a business. Evaluate whether ChatGPT recognises differences in digital maturity, implementation capacity, and outcomes.

**Limitation:** The sample is not representative of the entire SME population, results are self-reported, and the study is not restricted to e-Commerce. These percentages do not establish causal productivity gains or apply automatically to your chosen retailer.

**In-text citation:** (Bianchini & Lasheras Sancho, 2025)

## R2 — References supporting the platform evaluation section

### Prompt

Which references best support Section 2 (platforms), and how should I use them?

### Response

Mate, **F1 — OECD (2021) is the best reference in your bank for Section 2**. It provides a foundation for platform selection criteria. To verify specific features and costs, combine this source with the official documentation for Shopify, Amazon, and WooCommerce.

**I would use this combination:**

| Reference | What it supports | How to use it in your analysis |
|---|---|---|
| **F1 — [OECD: The Digital Transformation of SMEs](https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/the-digital-transformation-of-smes_bdb9256a-en/full-report/component-7.html)** | Platform benefits, market access, dependence, switching costs, and data-related risks. | Evaluate whether ChatGPT’s recommendation considers the company’s objectives and resources, in addition to features. |
| **[Shopify: plans and features](https://www.shopify.com/pricing)** | Included hosting, operations management, POS options, and differences between plans. | Check the recommendation of Shopify for a company wanting to integrate online and physical sales. Verify which features require additional costs. |
| **[Amazon: selling costs](https://sell.amazon.com/pricing)** | Selling-plan fees, category-based referral fees, and fulfilment options. | Evaluate whether expanding reach through the marketplace would also preserve the company’s margin. More sales do not automatically mean more profit. |
| **[WooCommerce: cost structure](https://woocommerce.com/pricing/)** | Free core, choice of hosting, and extension and payment-processing costs. | Check the distinction between flexibility, free software, and the total cost of operation. |

**F2 — OECD (2016) serves as a supporting source** if you discuss transparency, consumer protection, and data handling as selection criteria. It is more central to Section 3. [OECD: Consumer Protection in E-Commerce](https://doi.org/10.1787/9789264255258-en).

**How to use these sources without turning the section into a feature list**

Follow this sequence in each argument:

**ChatGPT’s claim → source evidence → your judgement → consequence for the business.**

Applying this to the response you received:

**1. Shopify: confirm suitability and identify conditions**

ChatGPT recommended Shopify for integrating physical and online stores. The documentation confirms the availability of features for these channels, but also shows differences between plans and additional services.

Your judgement can assess: **this recommendation makes sense for a store with physical and digital channels, but depends on the features it needs and its available budget**. The existence of a feature alone does not establish that the investment is worthwhile. [Shopify](https://www.shopify.com/pricing).

**2. Amazon: distinguish reach from profitability**

The response linked Amazon to market expansion and acknowledged additional costs. The fee documentation allows you to examine that qualification in greater depth.

Your critique could be: **the recommendation is plausible, but it still does not allow a decision without calculating the margin per product after referral fees, fulfilment, advertising, and returns**. This identifies a practical limitation of the response without incorrectly claiming that it ignored costs. [Amazon](https://sell.amazon.com/pricing).

**3. WooCommerce: evaluate the company’s capacity**

The response highlighted flexibility and maintenance requirements. The documentation confirms that the free core exists alongside other costs; update guidance also requires backups and testing.

Your judgement can assess: **WooCommerce better addresses customisation needs when the company can fund and maintain that solution**. Having technical freedom does not mean being able to use it efficiently. [WooCommerce costs](https://woocommerce.com/pricing/), [maintenance guidance](https://woocommerce.com/document/how-to-update-woocommerce/).

**Use the OECD to connect these three arguments:** platform selection should consider benefits, available resources, and dependence on the provider. This connection turns the comparison into an analysis of **strategic alignment**. [OECD, 2021](https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/the-digital-transformation-of-smes_bdb9256a-en/full-report/component-7.html).

Since your entire report is 800 words, select the points most relevant to your chosen scenario. Maintain a distinction when evaluating evidence: **official documentation confirms what the platform offers; vendor case studies illustrate applications but do not prove superiority or guaranteed outcomes**.

## R3 — How to conclude the report

### Prompt

Bro, how should I conclude?

### Response

Bro, to conclude the **report**, connect the six sections through one central idea: **the value of e-Commerce depends on the alignment between strategy, technology, and stakeholder needs.**

In around **70–90 words**, make three moves:

1. **Summarise the benefit:** integrated operations, customer reach, and data-informed decisions.
2. **Present your critical evaluation:** ChatGPT’s responses helped organise the topics, but subjective rankings and vendor-published cases require comparison with external sources.
3. **Close with a condition for success:** choose a suitable platform, gather requirements, apply the SDLC, and address privacy, security, and inclusion.

An example in English to adapt to the analysis you actually developed:

Overall, e-commerce systems can improve business operations when platform capabilities align with business strategy and stakeholder requirements. ChatGPT provided a useful starting point across the six topics, although its subjective rankings and reliance on vendor success stories required critical comparison with external evidence. Effective implementation depends on requirements gathering, an iterative SDLC, and attention to privacy, security, and inclusion. Emerging technologies offer further opportunities, but their value should be judged through measurable business outcomes and responsible use.

Avoid introducing a new reference or argument in the conclusion: it should bring together what you have already demonstrated in the body of the report.
