## 1. Selecting the case-study area

### Prompt

This will be my TECH1300 Assessment 2. Which use case do you think I will enjoy most, and which best matches my direction: software engineering for five years followed by lecturing for ten years?

### Response

ERP is the broadest match for that career direction because it involves large-system architecture, integrations, databases, APIs, permissions, workflows and multiple interdependent modules. SCM may, however, be more technically enjoyable if you like logistics, automation, optimisation, demand forecasting, tracking and real-time systems. CRM is closer to product, sales, marketing and user experience. ERP is the safest broad choice, while SCM is the most specialised and potentially engaging choice.

## 2. Comparing SCM with ERP

### Prompt

Is SCM genuinely competitive, or would I lose too much by choosing it?

### Response

Choosing SCM would not significantly weaken the alignment with software engineering or future teaching. It offers architecture and system integration, APIs, databases, event-driven processing, inventory and order tracking, automation, analytics, IoT, cloud, security and inter-organisational data reliability. Compared with ERP, the main trade-off is breadth: ERP spans finance, HR, procurement, inventory and operations, while SCM goes deeper into flows between suppliers, organisations, transport providers and customers. SCM is concrete, technically interesting and well suited to diagrams and innovative proposals.

## 4. Understanding the GridWave case

### Prompt

Show the case study translated into Portuguese and add brief notes to clarify the details.

### Response

The explanation identified GridWave Energy Solutions as a fictional Australian renewable-infrastructure company that designs, installs and maintains solar farms, wind turbines and battery storage systems across Australia and Southeast Asia. Its projects depend on solar panels from Asia, turbine components from Europe, battery systems from the United States and local civil and electrical contractors.

The central problem is fragmentation. GridWave uses an old ERP procurement module, spreadsheets for imports and exports, separate contractor software, email-based shipment coordination and no integrated risk-monitoring system.

The six business challenges were explained as follows:

1. **Global supply and geopolitical risk:** dependence on overseas suppliers creates exposure to trade restrictions, customs delays, exchange-rate changes and country risk.
2. **Long lead-time components:** turbine parts can require 6–12 months, while engineering changes are poorly coordinated with procurement.
3. **Project-schedule misalignment:** materials may arrive before sites are ready, creating storage costs, or arrive late and leave contractors idle.
4. **Compliance complexity:** environmental reports, sustainability certificates and audit evidence are handled manually.
5. **Supplier and contractor performance gaps:** there is no consolidated method for comparing reliability, quality and value, or linking invoices to delivery milestones.
6. **Capital-project budget overruns:** procurement costs are disconnected from financial forecasts and reconciled manually, leaving executives with limited real-time visibility.

GridWave's 2025–2029 goals are to implement a global cloud SCM platform, improve supply and geopolitical-risk monitoring, align procurement with engineering and site readiness, digitise ESG and compliance evidence, monitor contractor performance, reduce budget overruns by 20%, and provide real-time executive dashboards.

## 10. Understanding global concentration risk

### Prompt

Explain like a primary-school teacher: what does China's growing dominance in the global sustainable-energy market have to do with global risk?

### Response

Imagine that an entire school buys pencils from one shop. While the shop operates normally, everything is fine. If it closes, raises prices or stops delivering, the whole school has a problem. Renewable energy is similar: China produces a large proportion of the world's solar-panel and battery components. If trade disputes, port disruptions, tariffs or material shortages occur, many countries can be affected simultaneously. The issue is not that China is inherently bad; it is excessive dependence on one source. For GridWave, concentration increases the possible impact of delays, price rises and interruptions, supporting the need for supplier monitoring, risk forecasting and alternative sources.

## 24. SLA definition

### Prompt

What is an SLA?

### Response

SLA means **Service Level Agreement**. It is a contract defining what a cloud provider must guarantee, such as 99.9% availability, response times for failures, technical support and compensation when agreed service levels are not met. For GridWave, it would reduce the operational risk of losing access to the SCM platform during important activities.
