### Guide for developing the Future Trends section

**1. Define the main argument**

Begin by deciding what connects the trends. A suitable position is that emerging technologies can improve speed, automation and flexibility, but they also create new requirements for governance, integration and workforce skills.

The section should answer:

> How will these trends change the way organisations plan, select and implement Information Systems?

**2. Use Gartner to introduce AI-native development**

Explain that AI-native development platforms may accelerate software delivery and allow smaller teams to create more applications.

Do not only describe the technology. Connect it to a decision:

* **Planning:** organisations must identify suitable AI use cases and risks.
* **Selection:** AI platforms must be assessed for security, integration and human oversight.
* **Implementation:** organisations should begin with controlled pilots rather than immediate large-scale adoption.

Also note that the Gartner document provided is dated 2026, not 2025.

**3. Connect AI adoption with governance**

Explain that faster development can also increase the speed at which errors, insecure code or compliance problems are introduced.

The implication is that AI governance should be established before adoption. This may include:

* responsibility for AI decisions;
* acceptable-use policies;
* security controls;
* monitoring;
* human review;
* compliance requirements.

The critical point is that AI governance should be part of planning and implementation, not added after the system is deployed.

**4. Present cloud, APIs and events as supporting infrastructure**

Avoid explaining cloud, APIs and event-driven systems as separate trends.

Instead, show their relationship:

> AI and modern digital services depend on access to data and connections between different systems.

This creates an implication for selection. Organisations should evaluate whether a solution can:

* integrate with legacy and cloud systems;
* provide secure and documented APIs;
* support events or asynchronous communication;
* scale as demand grows;
* operate across hybrid environments.

**5. Use ACS and Deloitte to introduce the skills limitation**

Explain that advanced technologies cannot be adopted effectively when the organisation lacks skills in AI, cybersecurity, data and governance.

Connect this finding to implementation:

* assess existing capabilities;
* identify training needs;
* decide whether to recruit or use external partners;
* provide continuous upskilling;
* ensure business users can use AI responsibly.

The main insight is:

> A technology opportunity may become an implementation risk when the organisation lacks the skills to manage it.

**6. Bring the trends together**

Finish by showing that these trends are connected:

* AI creates opportunities for automation and faster development.
* Cloud and integration enable access to systems and data.
* Governance controls the new risks.
* Skills determine whether the organisation can use these technologies successfully.

### Suggested section order

1. Introduce AI as a major change in Information Systems.
2. Explain the opportunity created by AI-native development.
3. Discuss the need for AI governance and security.
4. Connect AI with cloud and system integration.
5. Use skills gaps to show a practical adoption limitation.
6. Finish with an implication for planning, selection and implementation.

### Review question

After writing the section, check:

> Have I only described the future technologies, or have I explained how they change an Information Systems decision?
